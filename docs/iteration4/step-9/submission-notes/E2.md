# E2 — The child's phenotype twin among 76,699 MGI mice: submission note

**Standing:** written 2026-09-24 from entry [150](../methodology/150-the-childs-phenotype-twin-among-mgi-mice.md); re-check before the submission is assembled.

## The finding, in one sentence

The child's HPO terms put a Bub1b mouse in the top ~1 % of all 76,699 MGI genotypes (~10× better
than IC-matched random terms, p 0.005–0.06), and the closest model is the **BubR1 hypomorph**
(H/H, H/L1002P) for the growth / muscle / perinatal half of the child and the **K243R/+
acetylation-deficient knock-in** for the tumour half — so the senolytic (D5) and SIRT2/NAD+ (D3)
rescues done in H/H mice are the ones to import, flagged "never tested with an approved drug".

## Where it goes in the Track 2 report

| report section | what to write there |
|---|---|
| §5 mechanism (Q14) | Independent of the genome: the child's 8 clinical terms alone pick BubR1-deficient mice from 76,699 genotypes (top ~1 %, ~10× chance). The best match is the *hypomorph* class (reduced amount of working BubR1), consistent with a missense + truncating compound het lowering BubR1 abundance — and the tumour half matches an allele that destabilises BubR1 (K243R, premature degradation, PMID 23878276). Both point to "too little stable BubR1", not a dominant-negative. Caveat: H086 not supported as pre-registered (rollups disagree). |
| §7 candidates / directions | D5 (new): senolytic clearance rescued the H/H twin (INK-ATTAC 22048312; p16 deletion 18516091); approved analogue dasatinib (+ quercetin, a supplement); mTOR from Sieben 2020 (31738183) is an observation only → sirolimus/everolimus as hypothesis. D3: SIRT2 overexpression extended H/H lifespan (24825348); NAD+ precursors (niacin approved; nicotinamide, NR/NMN supplements); SIRT2 does nothing on a wild-type background (38009412) — the effect is specific to low BubR1, i.e. to this child's class. Cautions: never suppress p19Arf / p53 (18516091, 23602569); SIRT2 also deacetylates the K250 site whose acetylation stabilises BubR1 (25285631) — the D3 route could cut both ways. |

(3) Carrier parents: the X753/+ (GTTA) carrier mouse shows late muscle wasting and shortened lifespan (23300461) — a surveillance point for heterozygous parents. |

## Claims it changes

- **H085** — suggested *supported* (see below). **H086** — suggested *inconclusive* (below).
- `ressources/track2/mechanism.json` D3 rationale says NMN "raised BubR1 abundance in vivo and
  extended lifespan in BubR1-hypomorphic mice (PMID 24825348)". The abstract attributes the
  **lifespan** gain to SIRT2 **overexpression** in H/H; NMN only raised BubR1 abundance, and the
  abstract does not say NMN was given to H/H mice. The sentence should be split.
- Iteration-2 report: "the deepest mechanistic lead rests on one mouse paper" — now the lead
  mouse is shown to be the child's phenotype twin (Phenodigm rank 768 / 76,699), and a
  second paper (38009412) bounds it (SIRT2 inert on a wild-type background).
- `docs/iteration4/step-9/research/data-sources.md` §5 labels `Bub1b<tm2.1Jvd>` "overexpression
  (PMID 23300461)". MGI and the abstract say it is the **GTTA / X753 truncating MVA knock-in**
  (Wijshake 2012); `<tm3.1Jvd>` is L1002P. (Research note, not a result — flag, do not edit.)
- New: `ressources/track2/model_interventions.tsv` feeds D3 and D5 (E7 grades D5).

## Limitations and what would change the conclusion

- H085 would weaken if an OWLSim/uPheno-based score (Monarch semsim `compare`) placed Bub1b
  genotypes outside the top 5 %, or if the 8-term Phenodigm p (0.060) is the honest one.
- H086 would flip to supported if the K243R/+ lead is an annotation artefact (its 14 MP terms
  are all tumours, which the reverse direction rewards); it would be refuted by a null-class
  genotype winning under any future MGI release.
- The intervention table is abstract-level; effect sizes need the full texts.
- Absence of kidney annotations may be absence of phenotyping.

**New dictionary terms** (for `docs/dictionary.md`, each after its dependencies):

- **MP (Mammalian Phenotype ontology)** — HPO's mouse counterpart: a DAG of mouse phenotypes.
  *Example:* MP:0001732 postnatal growth retardation. *Context:* entry 150; MGI annotations.
- **MGI genotype** — the exact allele pair(s) a mouse line carries, with its curated MP terms.
  *Example:* `Bub1b<tm1Jvd>/Bub1b<tm1Jvd>` (BubR1 H/H). *Context:* `MGI_PhenoGenoMP.rpt`, entry 150.
- **hypomorph (hypomorphic allele)** — an allele that still works, but less (lower amount or
  activity). *Example:* BubR1 H (`Bub1b<tm1Jvd>`), "low BubR1" mice (Baker 2004, PMID 15208629). *Context:* MGI allele
  attribute; H086.
- **knock-in allele** — an engineered allele carrying a specific sequence change (e.g. a patient
  mutation). *Example:* `Bub1b<tm3.1Jvd>` = L1002P. *Context:* entry 150.
- **SSSOM** — a TSV standard for term-to-term mappings between ontologies (predicate +
  justification per row). *Example:* HP:0000121 ↔ MP:0003197. *Context:* bridge tiers, entry 150.
- **uPheno** — the cross-species phenotype ontology that aligns HP, MP, ZP and others.
  *Context:* tier-1 logical and tier-2 fuzzy bridges, entry 150.
- **Phenodigm** — a mouse-model-to-disease similarity score combining the best and the average
  term match, each over the query's self-score. *Context:* secondary rollup, entry 150.
- **IC-matched null** — random term sets whose terms have the same information content as the
  real ones, to ask "is it these terms, or any terms this specific?". *Example:* p = 0.026.
  *Context:* entry 150.
- **senolytic** — a drug that selectively kills senescent (permanently arrested,
  inflammatory) cells. *Example:* dasatinib + quercetin. *Context:* D5, `model_interventions.tsv`.
- **INK-ATTAC** — a transgene that kills p16Ink4a-expressing cells when the mouse is given a
  dimerising drug (AP20187). *Context:* PMID 22048312; D5.
