# E5+E6 — Is each candidate safe *for this child*? His pharmacogenome and his own phenotypes: submission note

**Standing:** written 2026-09-24 from entry [152](../methodology/152-is-each-candidate-safe-for-this-child.md); re-check before the submission is assembled.

**Clinical framing.** Research exercise. Nothing here advises for or against giving any drug
to this child. Every row is a question for a treating team, with its evidence and its
uncertainty attached.

## The finding, in one sentence

The three statins Track 2 ranks first, plus hydroxychloroquine and vincristine, are flagged by
**both** independent sources — their guideline pharmacogene (SLCO1B1, G6PD) is a **no call**
in this child's variant-only callset, and their labels name his own skeletal muscle atrophy on
78–100 % of them against a matched base rate of 40 % — while the callset answers only
**1.6 %** of the pharmacogenomic positions, which turns the headline deliverable from a
recommendation into a **test request**.

## Limitations and what would change the conclusion

- **H090 would become a real answer** — not a qualified one — if the PGx positions were
  genotyped from the reads. That needs a single pass over the 67.68 GB read store, which the
  measured ~11 MB/s link makes a ≥ 1.7 h transfer; on a machine beside the data it is minutes.
  It is the single highest-value follow-up in this step.
- **H090 would be refuted** by a re-call showing SLCO1B1, ABCG2, CYP2C9, G6PD and UGT1A1 are
  all genuinely normal-function and that CYP3A5's call is an artefact of its one genotyped
  position.
- **H091 would weaken** under a narrower mapping. Dropping the two broadest terms
  (`Rhabdomyolysis`, `Weight decreased`) would cut both rates; the per-effect counts in
  `label_summary.json` let anyone recompute it. It would be refuted if a comparison set
  matched on ATC class rather than label breadth closed the gap.
- A label is a hazard statement, not an incidence. "96 % of atorvastatin labels name myopathy"
  says the regulator required the warning, not that 96 % of children on it get myopathy.
- FAERS cannot separate drug from disease: a child on vincristine has cancer, which itself
  causes muscle wasting.
- The regional density proxy is region-level, not per-position depth. A normally-called region
  can still hold a low-coverage hole at one PGx position.
- Confidence numbers here are a transparent triage heuristic, not a calibrated posterior.

**New dictionary terms** (for `docs/dictionary.md`, each placed after its dependencies):

- **pharmacogenomics (PGx)** — how a person's own DNA changes what a drug does to them: how
  fast they clear it, how much reaches the target, how likely a side effect is.
  *Example:* a poor metaboliser of CYP2C19 clears clopidogrel slowly. *Context:* E5, entry 152;
  the two-source safety table.
- **star allele (`*` allele)** — the naming scheme for pharmacogene variants: a numbered
  haplotype (a set of variants inherited together) rather than one position. *Example:*
  `SLCO1B1*5`. *Context:* PharmCAT's output unit; entry 152 never writes one, because a star
  allele is a genotype.
- **diplotype** — the pair of star alleles a person carries in one gene, one per chromosome
  copy. *Context:* PharmCAT's intermediate result; it stays in `build/`, entry 152.
- **metaboliser phenotype** — the *actionability word* a diplotype maps to: poor,
  intermediate, normal, rapid, ultrarapid. This, not the diplotype, is what a guideline keys
  on and what this repository is allowed to write down. *Example:* "CYP3A5: Poor Metabolizer".
  *Context:* entry 152; `ressources/track2/candidate_safety.tsv`.
- **CPIC (Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium)** — publishes the "given this
  metaboliser phenotype, prescribe like this" guidelines, graded A (act on it) to D.
  *Example:* SLCO1B1 and statins, level A (PMID 35152405). *Context:* entry 152; CPIC API.
- **PharmCAT** — open-source tool that reads a VCF, calls diplotypes for ~23 pharmacogenes and
  attaches the matching CPIC/DPWG/FDA guideline text. *Example:* v3.4.0, data 2026-07-13.
  *Context:* PMID 31306493; E5.
- **variant-only VCF** — a callset holding a record only where the sample differs from the
  reference; the ordinary output of a small-variant pipeline. The trap: a position with no
  record is *unknown*, not *reference*. *Example:* 1,204 of 1,226 PharmCAT positions absent
  here. *Context:* entry 152 E5.1; `ideation/data-findings.md` §2.3.
- **`--absent-to-ref`** — PharmCAT's flag that assumes every absent PGx position is
  homozygous reference. Its own authors label it DANGEROUS; entry 152 runs it only as a
  second arm and labels every result from it an assumption. *Context:* entry 152.
- **regional callability proxy** — a cheap way to ask whether an absent position is "matches
  the reference" or "a hole": compare the variant density the pipeline *did* call across the
  gene's region with the genome-wide density. *Example:* SLCO1B1 0.52×, ABCG2 0.00×.
  *Context:* introduced in entry 152; `figures/it4/step9-e56-coverage.svg`.
- **MedDRA (Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities)** — the controlled vocabulary drug
  labels and adverse-event reports are coded in; PT = preferred term, LLT = lower-level term.
  *Example:* PT 10029146 Nephrocalcinosis. *Context:* OnSIDES; `hpo_to_adverse_terms.tsv`.
- **adverse drug event (ADE) / labelled adverse effect** — an effect a regulator required the
  manufacturer to print on the product label. A hazard statement, not an incidence.
  *Example:* Myopathy on 382 of 397 atorvastatin labels. *Context:* E6, entry 152.
- **OnSIDES** — a machine-readable table of drug→adverse-effect pairs extracted from
  regulatory labels with a fine-tuned PubMedBERT (F1 0.90). *Example:* v3.1.1, 6.93 M rows,
  1,866 ingredients. *Context:* PMID 40179876; data CC BY 4.0.
- **FAERS (FDA Adverse Event Reporting System)** — the spontaneous-report database: anyone may
  file a report. A numerator with **no denominator**, so it shows signals, never rates.
  *Example:* 57 paediatric reports of muscular weakness with vincristine. *Context:* openFDA,
  entry 152 E6.3.
- **base rate (of a labelled effect)** — how often *any* comparable drug's label carries the
  effect. Without it, "statins cause myopathy" is uninterpretable. *Example:* 39.7 % of a
  matched comparison set. *Context:* entry 152 E6.2; `figures/it4/step9-e56-baserate.svg`.
- **matched comparison set** — controls chosen to resemble the cases on the main confounder,
  here the number of distinct adverse effects a drug's labels carry (a proxy for label
  verbosity and depth of study). *Example:* 5 nearest ingredients per candidate, 375 in all.
  *Context:* entry 152.
- **Wilson score interval** — a confidence interval for a proportion that stays inside 0 and 1
  even with a handful of observations, unlike the normal approximation. *Example:* 45/75 →
  48.7–70.3 %. *Context:* `mva.drugs.patient_safety.wilson_interval`, entry 152.
- **malignant hyperthermia susceptibility** — an inherited risk of a life-threatening reaction
  to certain general anaesthetics and succinylcholine, carried by RYR1 and CACNA1S variants.
  *Example:* RYR1 "Uncertain Susceptibility" in this child's observed calls. *Context:*
  entry 152 E5.2; an incidental finding, not a Track 2 candidate.
