# E4 — PRISM replication: do the leads survive a screen nobody curated for this case? — submission note

**Standing:** written 2026-09-24 from entry [151](../methodology/151-prism-replication-of-the-track2-leads.md);
re-check before the submission is assembled.

## The finding, in one sentence

Put through PRISM (1,522 approved and clinical compounds × 919 cancer cell lines, built for no
case in particular), the statin direction **reproduces weakly** (4 of 5 testable statins point
the predicted way, simvastatin p 0.019 and rosuvastatin p 0.039 raw, **none surviving
multiple-testing correction**, and lovastatin — the report's rank-1 molecule — is not measured
on enough rhabdomyosarcoma lines to test at all), chloroquine's aneuploidy selectivity **does
not reproduce** (p 0.315), dasatinib (D5) is the **99.4th percentile** of all 6,790 compounds on
the aneuploidy contrast, and 33 of 34 Mps1/Aurora inhibitors are *less* effective in
high-aneuploidy lines — an independent replication of Cohen-Sharir 2021 that puts a second leg
under the report's "do not weaken the checkpoint further" row.

## Limitations and what would change the conclusion

- **Coverage would change everything.** If the statins were run on PRISM's full line panel — or
  on a rhabdomyosarcoma panel at all — H087 would get a real test. As it stands, the strongest
  statin result rests on 3 lines. A targeted 14-line dose-response of lovastatin, simvastatin
  and rosuvastatin in RD / RH30 / RH4 / RH41 is the experiment that settles D4, and it is a
  week of bench work, not a screen.
- **Cancer lines are the wrong model.** A constitutional, mosaic, still-missegregating patient
  is not a line with a fixed aneuploid karyotype. Cohen-Sharir's own caveat — short-term drug
  exposure and long-term dependency point different ways — applies to every row here.
- **D3 has no readout in this instrument.** "Raise BubR1 abundance" is not a growth-inhibition
  phenotype; the nicotinamide/niacin/valproate/vorinostat nulls here are uninformative for D3
  and must never be quoted against it.
- **Dasatinib's signal is fragile**: q = 0.231 after correction, δ halves in the other release.
  A pre-registered re-test in a third screen (GDSC, CTRP) with the same code would settle it and
  costs nothing but a loader.
- **One-sided tests by design.** Every row carries its sign and effect size so an effect in the
  opposite direction is visible even though the p value cannot flag it.

**Dictionary terms to add or improve:**

- **PRISM (Profiling Relative Inhibition Simultaneously in Mixtures)** — a screen that pools
  DNA-barcoded cancer cell lines in one well, adds a drug, and reads how much of each line's
  barcode survives. *Example:* PRISM Repurposing 24Q2, 6,790 compound rows × 919 lines.
  *Context:* Corsello 2020 PMID 32613204; entry 151; figures `step9-e4-*`; related:
  DepMap, cell line, log fold change.
- **Log fold change (LFC), in a screen** — log₂ of a line's barcode abundance with drug over
  without; **more negative = the drug killed more of that line**. *Example:* bortezomib's median
  −4.19 across the screen. *Context:* PRISM 24Q2 primary, entry 151.
- **AUC (area under the dose-response curve)** — one number summarising a whole dose series;
  lower = more sensitive. *Example:* dasatinib median 0.694 in high-aneuploidy lines.
  *Context:* PRISM 20Q2 secondary, entry 151; the sensitivity metric that checks LFC.
- **Aneuploidy score (DepMap)** — how many chromosome arms a cell line has gained or lost,
  counted 0–39. *Example:* the screen's median is 20; the rhabdomyosarcoma lines' median is 21.
  *Context:* `OmicsSignatures.csv` 24Q4, Cohen-Sharir 2021; entry 151; related: aneuploidy, CIN.
- **Cliff's delta** — P(a member of group A ranks above a member of group B) minus the reverse;
  +1 total separation, 0 no ordering. A rank effect size that needs no distribution.
  *Example:* dabrafenib +0.670 for melanoma lines. *Context:* entry 151,
  `mva.drugs.screen_replication.cliffs_delta`; related: Mann-Whitney U.
- **Mann-Whitney U test** — asks whether values from one group tend to rank above another's,
  using only the ordering. *Example:* simvastatin, 3 RMS lines vs 528, one-sided p 0.0189.
  *Context:* entry 151; related: Cliff's delta, rank test.
- **Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) correction / adjusted p (q)** — testing many drugs at once produces
  small p values by chance; BH rescales them so that a stated share of the "hits" are expected
  to be false. *Example:* simvastatin p 0.0189 → q 0.132 over 24 candidates. *Context:* entry
  151; related: multiplicity, family.
- **Negative-control band** — the same test run on drugs with no tie to the question, used to
  show what "significant" looks like when nothing is there. *Example:* in the 14-line RMS
  contrast, 95 % of unrelated drugs fall within Cliff's δ ±0.7. *Context:* entry 151, figure
  `step9-e4-effects`.
- **Oncotree code** — the standardised cancer-type label DepMap tags each line with. *Example:*
  `ERMS` = embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, `ARMS` = alveolar. *Context:* `Model.csv` 24Q4, entry 151.
- **BRD id (Broad compound id)** — the Broad Institute's identifier for a compound batch, e.g.
  `BRD-K09416995-001-39-9`; the first two segments are the molecule, the rest the batch.
  *Example:* lovastatin is `BRD-K09416995` across releases. *Context:* entry 151,
  `mva.drugs.screen_replication_prism.core_broad_id`; the stable key for cross-release joins.
- **Rank normalisation (within line)** — replacing each cell line's responses by their
  percentile *within that line*, so a line that is simply easy to kill stops looking
  drug-selective. *Example:* it halves rosuvastatin's RMS effect. *Context:* entry 151,
  `ResponseMatrix.rank_normalised`.
